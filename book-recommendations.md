# Book Recommendations — Agent Notes

**This file holds the rules. It holds nothing per-title.** Compact facts with
evidence, not narrative — full score history lives in [books-read.md](books-read.md).
Every bullet must trace to a specific book and score.

Five things run on these rules, and each owns exactly one kind of write:

| | Owns | Invalidated by |
|---|---|---|
| `/book-log` | scores in `books-read.md`, the rules here, and **every recomputation** of a prediction after a rule moves | a rule here moving |
| `/book-screen` | discovery, in two tiers: `book-triage.jsonl` (every record pulled, machine class, rough band) and then `book-cache.json` access facts, `book-rejects.jsonl`, `book-media.json`, **first** predictions for what it promotes | time passing |
| `/book-deep` | a sourced re-derivation of one candidate's prediction, marked `depth: deep`, with its per-axis evidence in `book-deep.jsonl` | a rule here moving (re-priced by `/book-log` from the dossier) |
| `/book-suggest` | the Snoozed list below, and nothing else | — |
| `index.html` | nothing — read-only viewer (`./serve.ps1`) | — |

**Three tiers, and only the last is an opinion worth quoting.** Between the two below
sits [book-ai-rank.jsonl](book-ai-rank.jsonl): a persisted 1-5 plausibility score per work
from a cheap model, keyed to a hash of `tools/ai-rank-prompt.md` so it is computed once and
never recomputed until that prompt changes. It answers "is this worth twenty minutes", not
"would he like it". A `3` there means *the model did not recognise the book* — never read it
as a negative.

**Two tiers, and only one of them is an opinion.** [book-triage.jsonl](book-triage.jsonl)
holds a row for every record ever pulled from the catalogue with a machine class and a
rough band — `conf: auto`, meaning a regex looked at the title, author and publisher and
nothing else. A band there is **not** a prediction and must never be quoted as one; a
`drop` there is *not pursued, revisit on request*, never a reject. Titles that clear the
promotion gate get the expensive treatment and appear in the three files below. This exists
because a sweep once pulled 16,687 records, recorded 67, and lost the rest.

Predictions live in [book-estimates.jsonl](book-estimates.jsonl) with the
dependencies each was derived under, counted in [book-revs.json](book-revs.json).
Changing a rule below therefore has a mechanical consequence: `/book-log` bumps
that rule's counter, greps the estimates for it, and rescores exactly the subset
that named it. So **rewording a rule is a real action, not a note** — and a rule
nothing depends on is a rule that will never be applied. **A changed score reaches the
estimates another way** (added 2026-10-29, on a rerank that moved 28 scores at once): each
line's `anchors` records which read-book scores cited in its `why` the range was set against,
and `python tools/anchors.py rescore` moves exactly those ranges and rewrites the cited numbers.

**A prediction is anchored to read books and to the rules here — never to another
candidate** (2026-08-26). Candidates do not mention each other: an estimate
calibrated against another estimate is a guess anchored to a guess, and both move
together when either turns out wrong. Every `why` therefore argues from scores in
[books-read.md](books-read.md) and from the axes below, in its own words, even
where that means two volumes by one author repeating the same reasoning. Sequence
and edition notes — read volume 1 first, this story also sits inside that
collection — are *facts*, so they belong in `book-cache.json` as `flags`, where
naming a sibling title is correct. Enforced by `python tools/crossrefs.py`, which
came out of 22 lines derived the other way, one of them arguing from "451 read"
when *Fahrenheit 451* is itself an unread candidate.

**This file must not grow with the log.** The log is heading past 100 books, so
evidence cites the two or three *strongest* exemplars for an axis — ideally a
controlled pair, same author or series — never every book that touches it. When
adding a book would turn a citation into a list, replace a weaker one instead.

## Scoring
**The reader scores out of 10, in whole numbers** (changed 2026-10-29; the letter bands are gone). `books-read.md` holds what he gave, with older rows keeping the decimal they were converted with (9.2). **Everything in this file, in the estimates and in every `why` stays on 0-100**, which is his score times ten: a 9 is 90 here, and `tools/readlog.py` is the only converter. What he is shown, in the viewer and in any proposal, is whole numbers out of 10. In his terms: 10 almost perfect · 9 enjoyed very much · 8 a nice read · 7 ok · 5-6 disliked, 5 in his own words "not liked much but not disliked much" (2026-10-30) · 4 and below actively disliked.

**A DNF can be left unscored** (ruled 2026-10-29: "some of the books are still quite good and not finished because I was not interested, but I do not want to have them with the books I did not like"). Those books sit in their own table in `books-read.md`. They count as read, and why he stopped is evidence about what loses him, but **they are never cited as a score or as a dislike**. Five went there on the ruling: *Problém tří těles*, *Guards! Guards!*, *The Colour of Magic*, *The Gunslinger* and *The Diary of a Young Girl*.

**An absolute rating, not a ranking — any number of books may share a score** (2026-08-22). Equal scores mean equal liking. Row order among ties is arbitrary: never read a preference out of it, never nudge a score to avoid a collision, never offer to break a tie.

**A score is peak minus pushbacks, not an average.** A modest book with no downsides ties an ambitious one with several. Evidence: *The Hobbit* 84 ("nothing great, but no downsides") = *LOTR* 84 ("much greater ideas, but more pushbacks") — a deliberate tie, not unfinished business. So don't assume the ambitious pick is the better bet.

**7 and 8 are acceptable outcomes, not failures** — don't optimise only for the top. An occasional 7 to expand horizons is explicitly welcome. Only 6 and below (sub-65 here) is a thing to avoid.

## Filters

**Seven bars, and one of them is now conditional** (count corrected 2026-09-02 — this line still said five after "sex as the plot" had been added, and `axis:same-sex-romance` makes seven): no readable route · an unclear opening · real-world occult on the page · detective/mystery · infidelity as the premise · sex as the plot · a same-sex romance as the premise. **The opening bar is the conditional one:** it is absolute for a book proposed cold and mitigable by reading the premise first — see that bullet. Everything else is graded — flag it and let the reader decide, never silently exclude.

**A hard exclusion is inherited by any sequel that depends on the excluded book** (reader's rule, 2026-08-31: "if the book is hard excluded, also the sequels should"). **The unit is DEPENDENCY, not the series** — and that distinction is not pedantry, it is forced by the evidence: *Mort* 83 is volume four of a series whose volume one was abandoned in its opening, and it is the best result this author has in the log, so "same series" cannot be the test or the rule would have excluded it. The test is one question: **can this book be read without the excluded one?** If the plot continues it, or the lead or premise is introduced there and assumed rather than re-established, the exclusion carries and the book is `blocked`, not given a low range — a range says *unlikely*, and these are *unreachable*. If the book restarts with its own lead and premise, it does not inherit. **An `access` reject does not propagate as an exclusion** — it is a dated fact, so the sequel stays live with a note that its route is missing. **A missing predecessor is a high risk** (reader's rule, 2026-10-29: "if you have missing prerequisites it should be as high risk"): a hard predecessor that is not on the shelf, or is rejected on access, gets a `prereq` link of kind `absent` (or `rejected` with `inherits: false`), and the sequel carries `axis:sequels` in `risks`, so it is never proposed as if it could be read in order. The links live in `book-cache.json` as `prereq` (added the same day, because until then reading order existed only as prose and nothing could join on it); `need: hard` is what triggers inheritance. **First application removed 13 books from the queue**, and only four of them were the author this came up about — see Open questions for what the sweep could and could not see.

**A series is read whole, so it is proposed whole** (ruled 2026-10-25: "I do not read single titles from series, so the series should be proposed as a whole"). One entry per series, labelled as a series, never a single volume and never two volumes of one series side by side. Its range is the mean of its volumes' ranges, and a volume that fails a hard filter blocks the series. Membership lives in `book-series.json`; the catalogue's own series field is often a publisher's line. This governs presentation, not scoring: each volume keeps its own estimate, and the sequel rules below still price each one.

**Clearing a filter is not a reason to propose** (2026-08-26). The four bars are exclusions, not endorsements: "short, in Czech, no occult" describes a book that has merely failed to disqualify itself. Every proposal needs a *positive* case on the graded axes — ideas, protagonist, motives, worldbuilding, discovery — stated in its own words. An estimate whose `why` is a list of filters passed is not an estimate.

**Severity among the graded objections** (from the ranked disliked band, 2026-08-19, revised 2026-08-22 and on the 2026-10-29 rerank): occult ≫ crude sexual content > incoherent premise = unrootable or passive protagonist = sustained bleakness. **The rerank collapsed the middle of the order:** *Divergent* 50 and *Mortal Engines* 50 (premise), *Ballad* 50 and *Oliver Twist* 50 (protagonist) and *1984* 50 (bleakness, its only named cause) now share one score, so no order among those three can be read off the log. *Proměna* 40, where a passive lead and bleakness fired together, sits under all of them. **Prophecy handling is unplaced:** its only data point, *Já, robot* 50, carries two other causes. Two adjustments when *proposing*: incoherent premise is one of only two objections known to end a book unfinished in the first chapters (*Mortal Engines*), so weight it first within that tie; and violence and swearing are not objections at all within limits. **`axis:same-sex-romance` is deliberately absent from this ordering** — it is heavy on the reader's own account but its only data point is confounded with two other deductions on the same book, so it has a rank nobody can place yet.

- **Hard — no confusing/unclear opening. Still *confusion*, not slowness — but confusion has two shapes, and the second was added 2026-08-28.** **Shape one — too many threads:** several plot lines starting at once before any is legible. Evidence: *Guards! Guards!* (DNF, unscored) (abandoned p. 15, ~6 threads at once), *The Colour of Magic* (DNF, unscored). **Shape two — no context for the thread you get:** dropped into an action already underway, followable sentence by sentence and meaning nothing, because who these people are, what the invented terms denote and why any of it matters have not been established. Evidence: *Čaropisci*, abandoned in chapter one — a heist already running, with scriving, sigils and four merchant houses arriving unexplained; the reader's own words were "starts from nowhere, with nothing immersing me", and on being shown what the chapter actually contains he placed it as "lots happening, none of it meant anything yet". *The Gunslinger* (DNF, unscored) is the same shape. So is *Permafrost* (DNF p. 40, unscored), behind a premise stated within four pages: at p. 40 of a short novella he still knew almost nothing about the leads or what they wanted. The premise is not the context; the people are. **What kills is missing context, not missing incident.** An action opening is therefore no defence, and "the first chapter has a heist in it" is precisely the argument that failed. **A legible-but-uneventful opening is NOT this filter**: that reading of *Čaropisci* was put to the reader and rejected, and *The Silmarillion* 70 is quiet, archaic and cold and still scored an ok 7. What he wants is one concrete starting situation **he has the context to read**, and to be invested immediately. **An early DNF does not by itself diagnose this filter** — *Mortal Engines* 50 was dropped in the first chapters on premise logic with no complaint about clarity (2026-08-22). Two objections end a book that early — this filter and premise logic; ask which.

  **MITIGABLE — and this is the most useful thing the log has learned about the filter (2026-09-02).** *Čaropisci* was gone back to and read to the end (scored **67**, lowered to **50** in the 2026-10-29 rerank), and the reader priced the opening himself: *"if only this it would be still around 80, because I would read about it online."* Same book, same opening: **a DNF cold, and only a few points off with a plot summary in hand.** That follows from the diagnosis rather than contradicting it — the failure is missing context, and looking the context up supplies it. Three consequences. **(a) A book whose only objection is this filter is proposable with a read-the-premise-first note, not excluded.** **(b) Survived, it costs a few points, not a tier** — the only figure the log has for that. **(c) It still stands unmitigated for anything proposed cold**, because the first read really did stop at chapter one. **What this does not license:** shape one has no such measurement — *Guards! Guards!* (DNF, unscored) and *The Colour of Magic* (DNF, unscored) were never retried — so the mitigation is recorded for **shape two only**, and a many-threads opening is still a bar.
- **Hard — no occult, in any setting, but only the Source test and staged human sacrifice still veto (re-ruled 2026-10-24).** Two tests. *Source:* a system drawn from actual occultism or real gods fails — witchcraft, spellcasting in that mould, séance, divination in the witch-or-seer mould, necromancy, demon-summoning from real grimoire tradition, sacrifice or prayer to real deities. **Still a veto whatever the severity** (confirmed 2026-10-24), and resemblance counts: "if it's mechanical it could be plausible explanation to keep it normal, but if too similar to occult it should be still vetoed." *Depiction:* **a rite of an INVENTED system enacted on the page is graded by severity, no longer a veto** — "Depends on the severity. But human sacrifice is almost never acceptable" (2026-10-24), confirmed for any staged rite, a working rite performed by the heroes included. So **staged human sacrifice vetoes and every other staged invented rite grades.** **A STATED CONVENTION, not a measurement:** `light` −2 (an invented faith's ceremony, incidental or doing nothing: a blessing, a funeral offering of animals), `moderate` −5 (a rite that works, staged once or twice: an invented religion's foretelling, answered), `heavy` −10 (rites central to the plot or to how the leads' power works, performed repeatedly, or raising the dead), applied to both ends of a range; replace it when a book with a staged invented rite is read. **The escape is mechanism, and it costs nothing:** a system operated like technology or engineering, with declared rules and no invocation, devotion or priesthood — Allomancy, Asimov's mentalics, salvaged "elf-magic" that is really pre-collapse tech. **Check the whole system, not its daily use (2026-09-02): ask how power is CONFERRED.** Origin myth, ascension, apotheosis and initiation are where an engineered-looking system hides its rite, late in the book where a blurb-level pass clears them; Bennett's Founders scriving left the escape list for exactly that, because creating a hierofant "require something like human sacrifice — quite similar to some ritual". **A rite that is the system's origin or elevation lore, not something the story stages, is a heavy graded deduction even when it is a human sacrifice** — *Čaropisci* 50, whose hierofant sacrifice he named a significant downside and still finished the book. So for human sacrifice the line is what the narrative stages. Evidence for the Source veto: *Harry Potter* 20 and *The Witcher* 30, two of the three lowest scores, plus *Percy Jackson* 57 — and *Havran* 10, the lowest, where the narrator questioning the bird about the dead Lenore is divination in the historical-occult mould. *Havran* was read and scored rather than dropped: the filter governs what gets **suggested**, not what he can finish. Passing cases: ***Licanius* 93-100** — Essence and kan, invented, declared and used constantly on the page, called "interesting", no occult objection: the first read book to clear the mechanism escape, and it tops the log. Also *LOTR* 84 (wizards, no real-occult analogue) and Narnia 60 (a witch, and a sacrifice at the Stone Table, with no occult objection recorded).

- **A filter stated as two tests must say how they combine — the ambiguity is invisible until a book lands in the gap.** It surfaced 2026-08-26 on *Divine Cities* against *Jonathan Strange* (invented systems, rites enacted on the page), and again 2026-10-24 when three deep dives drew the staged-rite line three ways (*Elantris*, *Levá ruka tmy*, *Půl krále*). **Now: Source alone vetoes; Depiction grades by severity, except staged human sacrifice, which vetoes.** Estimates blocked on an invented staged rite are re-derived under that; a title reject on that ground reopens only through `/book-screen` reconsider.
- **Hard — no detective/mystery novels.** Deliberately avoided, so few have been read; the absence of data is self-selection, not a gap (2026-08-19). Watch for *investigation-shaped subplots* in eligible books too — flag rather than drop (e.g. *Leviathan Wakes*, half of which is a missing-person case).
- **Hard — infidelity as the premise of the book (ruled 2026-08-27).** **Scope is marriage or engagement only**: a betrayal between unmarried partners is ordinary relationship drama and does not touch this axis. **An arranged betrothal is not an engagement for this axis** (ruled 2026-10-24: "Arranged betrothal does not mean infidelity if in that arranged marriage is not infidelity"): a lead promised by others who loves someone else is not unfaithful; an affair inside the marriage once it is made still counts. **Premise level is always a reject, whatever the framing** — "when the premise of the book is infidelity then reject it always". Premise means *remove the infidelity and there is no book*: the main plot, the central relationship, or the engine of the conflict. So *Anna Karenina*, *Madame Bovary* and *Effi Briest* are **rejects, not low scores** — ruining the adulterer is not a defence, because the reader is still being asked to spend a novel inside the affair. **Literary reputation earns nothing here**, consistent with "clearing the filters is not a reason to score well". This superseded a first draft the same day that made the veto conditional on the affair being *celebrated and unpunished*, and it is the simpler rule as well as the stricter one: framing no longer enters the veto at all, so the test is prominence alone and a blurb usually settles it (budget 2, not 3, and the ending need not be established). Where premise vs subplot is genuinely unclear, grade it as a subplot and say so — a wrong reject is unrecoverable.
- **Infidelity below premise level — graded on two dimensions that multiply, each severe alone.** *Prominence* (incidental / subplot) against *framing* (condemned or destructive / neutral / approving — celebrated, presented as liberation, or simply unpunished): an approving incidental moment lands in the 50s, a neutral subplot 45-55, an approving subplot 30-40. **Two open items:** no book in `books-read.md` anchors this axis — 49 scored books and not one note mentions infidelity — so these bands are the reader's stated intent rather than derived from a real score; and the 270 estimates already recorded were never screened for it. Note also that this axis grades on *amount* while the sexual-content axis below deliberately does not; that difference is intentional.

A relationship novel where the sex is one thread among the characters' lives stays graded; a book whose *theme* is desire or sexuality while its plot is about something else stays graded. Tasteful handling is no defence at premise level, exactly as condemning the affair is no defence under the infidelity filter. **Filter id is `axis:sexual-content`** — `axis:sexual-treatment` remains the graded crudeness axis below it, and the two must not be merged: one asks how much of the book this is, the other asks how it is handled. **Scope note, stated honestly:** no book in `books-read.md` anchors this filter — every sexual objection in the log sits on a book whose plot is about something else, so the bands here are the reader's stated intent, not derived from a real score. **Corrected 2026-08-28:** *Artemis* 60 and *Ready Player One* 40 are crudeness deductions and can be quoted; *Válka starého muže* 87 and *Red Rising* 95 are both low-volume rows that test nothing; and *The Witcher* 30 is confounded with the occult filter and cannot be quoted at all — see the crudeness axis below. ## The heaviest axis — does it hold up under thought?

Unifies the profile across genres. Three faces, each with its own evidence: *premise* — consequences worked out and reveals set up (*PHM* 100, astrophage's consequences followed through; *Golden Son* 92, the Fitchner-as-Ares reveal earned rather than sprung); *structure* — the parts connect to each other (the Tolkien gradient *Hobbit* 84 / *LOTR* 84 / *Silmarillion* 70 / *Unfinished Tales* 60 "fragmented and not aligned", mirrored by *Hitchhiker's* 83 vs its shapeless sequels 60); *fact* — real-world claims must be true (*Seeking Allah* 100, which weighs what is believable; *Hraničářův učeň* 50-70 by volume, false historical claims stated as fact).

**The one principle: the author must follow the rules he lays down** (the reader's formulation, 2026-08-22). Self-consistency with declared commitments, *not* an external realism audit. Nothing obliges an author to explain anything — but whatever he asserts, foregrounds or undertakes to explain becomes binding. Five forms, one breach:

1. **Declare a capability → the world must carry its consequences.** FTL or magic means the society is visibly different for it — *"then there would be colonization, the society would change"*.
2. **Undertake to explain → the explanation must hold.** Choosing to explain creates the obligation; silence would not have.
3. **Take a society as your subject → it must cohere.** *Divergent* 50 — the factions and genes *are* the subject.
4. **Declare a resource scarce or sacred → it must behave that way.** Invented resources are therefore *not* free.
5. **Declare a trait in a character → it must be shown.** *Divergent*'s Erudite are billed as intelligent and never written so. Same rule, not a separate axis (2026-08-22).

**What is actually binding — four scoping rules:**

- **Where nothing is declared, reality binds — set by the tech level the book *presents*, not the date it is set in.** *Mortal Engines* 50 "starts in the real world in the future, but... the science did not go much further, so the real world logic should still apply"; escape needs *"some indication that now is something different"*. This is the asymmetry with *Ender's Game* 87, which reads genuinely advanced and gets its battle station free.

  **One application of this rule lives on its own axis:** where a book gives no in-world reason, *intellectual* ability between the sexes is bound by reality like anything else undeclared, so all the cleverest characters being women is the undeclared case breached — while *physical* difference is a real-world fact a book gets credit for respecting. Both belong to `axis:sex-portrayal` under Preferences, because the reader's operative objection there turned out to be about portrayal rather than world-logic.
- **Jurisdiction is per world, and cross-world duty scales with the traffic — which has a direction.** *"Real world needs to be bound by real world rules... The magic world has it's own rules."* Check each direction separately and ask whether the declared mechanism covers *that* direction. **Narnia 60** owes nothing either way — a wardrobe now and then is negligible. **Harry Potter 20** is fine outbound, because secrecy is a declared, enforced institution that *earns* magic's absence from mundane life — the principle working in an author's favour — but breaches inbound: *"there are muggles accepted to the school of magic"*, arriving yearly with modern knowledge that wizarding society absorbs none of. Secrecy seals outbound influence; it cannot seal what immigrants bring in. **Generalisation:** for hidden-world and portal fiction, find who crosses over and stays, and ask what they bring.
- **The obligation scales with how precisely the thing is declared — the author sets his own bar** (hedged "i think"). Narnia's magic is "not declared as something very understandable and logical, so it's not that big of a problem"; Tolkien's unsystematised magic likewise costs nothing. This does **not** conflict with "consequences, not rules": rules add *obligation*, not bonus. Sanderson passes by meeting the bar he set, not by setting one.
- **Exemption follows how hard the book *leans* on the thing, not what it is nominally about.** *Harry Potter* 20 is a school story and still takes a worldbuilding hit, because magic is the medium of every scene. *Hunger Games* 89 merely mentions Panem's economics; *Ender's Game* 87 uses its station as a place events happen in. Lean, not aboutness — so don't grant the exemption just because a book is character-led.

**Scope in time: an answer anywhere in the book counts.** *"If it's explained further in the book it's ok."* So "explained later" is a valid defence — but verify it, because **the reader verifies**: he searched online before abandoning *Mortal Engines*. A sequel does not count.

**Weight:**
- **Graded, never a bar.** The deduction tracks how *central* the breach is and how *badly wrong*, together — neither alone predicts anything, and physical and social implausibility weigh the same.
- **It adds points, but a flat protagonist outranks it** (settled by *Artemis* 60, whose world-logic was explicitly fine and which landed 40 under *Project Hail Mary* 100 — lowered from 76 to 69 on 2026-08-27 for the same two reasons and to 60 in the 2026-10-29 rerank, the strongest single case in the log). **Never predict a book up on worldbuilding when the lead looks thin.** Same shape against story density (Tolkien, good world, capped at 84) and in the Ender sequels (better world than *Ender's Game* 87, yet #3-5 at 81).
- **It can end a book by itself in the first chapters** — one of only two DNF causes, needing no supporting fault.
- **Positively confirmed, not only inferred from failures** (volunteered 2026-08-22): *Hunger Games* ("thought through and makes sense"), *Ender's Game* ("very good"), both Weir novels ("awesome"), *Red Rising* and Tolkien all named as good worldbuilding — five authors, 82 to 100. **Six now, and the sixth states the premise face as a positive rather than a defect** (*Válka starého muže* 87, volunteered 2026-08-28): "there weren't much of the characters just having luck and the author saving the plot when it was useful," alongside a world that stays consistent through three shifts of perspective. **Authorial rescue is the same breach as a sprung reveal** — the author declaring stakes and then not honouring them — so it is this axis, not a separate one, and it is now the only face with evidence from both directions: *Golden Son* 92 for the reveal earned, this for the rescue refused. **Seventh, and the highest-scoring evidence the axis has:** *Licanius* 93-100 — "the world made sense and the uncovered plot twists were consistent with the rest of the book", held across three volumes and a time loop closed in the last. Failures: *Divergent* 50, *Mortal Engines* 50, *Harry Potter* 20, *Hraničářův učeň* 50-70 (false historical claims as fact), *Unfinished Tales* 60 and the Hitchhiker sequels 60 (structure face).

## Preferences (ordered by weight of evidence)
- **Motives over action** — page time on what characters want and why beats page time on what they do; set-piece action with no character revelation is dead weight. Four authors, all controlled and all agreeing: Collins *Mockingjay* 91 > *Hunger Games* 89 > *Catching Fire* 83 ("arena for its own sake"); Weir *PHM* 100 > *Martian* 82; Brown *Red Rising* 95 > *Golden Son* 92 (docked for leaning on action); Card *Speaker* 90 > *Ender's Game* 87 > *Shadow* 79. **In his own words, 2026-10-30: "I am not interested much about what happens, but about the people"** — said of *Střepy Země* (DNF p. 30, unscored), put down as too descriptive and "only something like scifi history". Description and a world's history are the same dead weight as action when no person's wants are on the page.
- **The protagonist must be interesting *and* worth rooting for** — two failure modes, and **this beats worldbuilding when they compete**. *Not rootable:* a lead acting as a villain or sliding into evil, however well written — *Sunrise on the Reaping* 87 (Haymitch) vs *Ballad* 50 (Snow), same author, world and form, **37 points on the protagonist alone** (22 before the 2026-10-29 rerank lowered *Ballad* from 65). **The direction of travel decides it, not the past** (2026-10-24). Caeden in *Licanius* 93-100 learns he was the series' great enemy, and the reader's words on why that worked: "I liked that he understood what he was and decided not to be the man he was before". A dark past faced and refused is not the *Ballad* failure; it is what the log's top series was liked for. *Not interesting:* flat even when likeable and competent — *Artemis* 60. **Ordinary is not flat** (promoted 2026-10-24 after two under-predictions). Never dock a lead for being an everyman, generic or unremarkable: *Válka starého muže* 87 was predicted [66, 76] on "everyman narrator", and *Stín věcí ztracených* 93 [72, 82] on "a generic lead". In both, what made the lead interesting (ordinariness written well; a past being uncovered) is invisible from outside. Flatness has to show in the text itself, as no interiority or one note (Jazz in *Artemis*), and is never inferred from the lead's type. **A lead whose own past is the open question is a positive:** uncovering who Caeden was, with the uncertainty left open, was the reader's single favourite element. Weir is the controlled case, **100 > 82 > 60** with world-logic strong throughout, though Artemis also carries a content deduction, so not all of that gap is the lead. **A third failure mode, and it is about flaws (*Čaropisci* 50, 2026-09-02): "Both men/women characters and mainly main characters should have flaws to overcome."** A lead handed no fault to work against is the *not interesting* mode arriving from a different direction — nothing to root **for**, because nothing is being overcome. It applies hardest to the leads. He rated *Čaropisci* "not bad" on this and "quite liked most of the main characters", so on this axis it is a check to run rather than a deduction taken, and `axis:protagonist` was **not** bumped for it. The *one-sided* version of the same pattern is a separate, priced axis — see `axis:sex-portrayal`.
- **A one-sided competence contrast between the sexes — graded, and the magnitude scales with the contrast (new axis `axis:sex-portrayal`, 2026-09-02).** The reader's own formulation, given when asked whether this should reach existing estimates: **"The main problem is when men are shown as dumb/incompetent and women as good/clever. The stronger the contrast the bigger the score decrease."** So the object is the *contrast*, not a count of who is competent — a book full of capable women costs nothing if the men are written as capable too. **What clears it:** the top clever characters being mixed, or an in-world reason given for why they are not. **What earns credit rather than costing:** respecting physical difference. "For woman to be good in fighting she usually needs to be creative and somehow be good even without big strength — this is fine in the Čaropisci." **What costs:** intellectual ability is equal in reality, so a world where the best scriver, the cleverest character and the leader of all the clans are all women with no reason given is the undeclared-reality case breached, and it costs more the more the men are diminished to make the point. **Anchor, and its limit:** *Čaropisci* 50 is the only data point and it is a *low* one — "in this book it is not that visible", "not bad, just a warning" — so the axis has a direction, a stated gradient and no measured high-contrast case. Do not quote a magnitude. **A stated dock convention, not a measurement, so the axis can be screened at all:** `none` 0 · `mild` −2 · `moderate` −5 · `strong` −10 on both ends of a range. It lives in `tools/reader-profile.md` so ten screeners cannot each invent their own scale, and it goes the moment a real high-contrast book is read. **SCREENED 2026-09-02 over all 248 candidates at or above the propose line — the whole point of the exercise, since below the line the answer changes no decision.** Result: **233 none, 13 mild, 2 moderate, no strong, no unknown — 6%**, and the plausibility of that base rate is the main reason to trust it; a screen that found this everywhere would be measuring the screener. **Four books dropped out of the proposable set:** *Lekce chemie* [71,80]→[66,75] and *Má sestra je sériový vrah* [70,80]→[65,75] on the two moderates, *Pravidla hry* and *Sucho* on mild docks from the line itself. **Two caveats, both real.** 57 of the 233 `none` verdicts carry **no citation** — canonical titles cleared from a screener's own knowledge, so "no evidence found" rather than "checked and clean". And nothing under the propose line was screened, so the axis is examined on 249 of 1,445 rows.
- **Wants higher things to think about** — a book should leave ideas behind, not just events; the most common reason a competent book stalls in the 60s. Evidence: Narnia 60 and *Hraničářův učeň* 50-70 marked down for lacking them; *Licanius* 96, *Seeking Allah* 100 and *The Little Prince* 82 rated on their ideas. *Licanius* is the first case where the ideas come out of an invented world: fate, belief and time, questioned by the characters inside the setting's own rules.
- **Discovery under necessity: an unknown the lead has to explore, and no telling what he will find (new axis `axis:discovery`, 2026-10-24).** The reader's words on *Project Hail Mary* 100, volunteered: "There was unknown world to explore, there was the need to explore it and there was not clear what he will find", where the unknown was both his own lost memories and the alien system. The same day he named the same thing as what he loved in *Licanius* 93-100: uncovering who Caeden was, "and having the uncertainty". **How the lead comes not to know does not matter** (ruled the same day: "The without memories does not need to be strictly there"): lost memory is one way, arriving in a different world or time period is another, and simply never having been told is a third, as with the outcast heroine of *Horizon Zero Dawn*, who "is just without an information about the world". The two strongest cases happen to pair lost memory with an unknown world; the pairing is not required and earns nothing extra. Three conditions, taken from his sentence: *an unknown* that is central (a world the lead does not understand, possibly his own past too); *a need*, so the lead must understand it to survive or to finish what he has to do, and exploring is the plot rather than sightseeing; *an open outcome* whose answer, when it comes, fits everything before it (the reveal face of the heaviest axis). Whether all three are required is untested; until a book separates them, a missing one prices as partial. **An answer withheld or not yet written forfeits the lift:** a mystery the book declines to explain, or an unfinished series whose central question is still open, is a risk rather than a lift. **It does not rescue a book failing elsewhere:** *Narnia* 60 and *Harry Potter* 20 are both a hidden world entered, and both sit low on other axes. So does *Problém tří těles* (DNF at p. 200, unscored). Its uncovering, the simulation, was the part he liked, and the book still went down on its passive lead. Being the first discovery book logged since the axis, it tests direction only, not size. Weir is consistent with it without isolating it: *The Martian* 82 has the need and no unknown, but the reader's own reasons for the eighteen-point gap were Rocky and the sacrifice. **Not the detective filter:** the unknown is the world or the lead's own self, never a culprit; a book whose satisfaction is who did it stays under that filter. **Credit it once:** a lead whose own past is the open question is priced here, not again under the protagonist axis. **Size, a STATED CONVENTION and not a measurement:** central with all three conditions met, about +4 at both ends of the range; partial (present but not the spine, or one condition missing), +2; one step lower when the lead is the open doubt, since the uncovering is felt through the lead and both evidence cases had leads he praised. The only calibration is direction: the uncovering series was under-predicted by 11 to 20 points, and the docks that did not fire explain part of that, not how much. Check the size against the next discovery book logged. Corroborated outside books, unscored so it weighs nothing: the game *Horizon Zero Dawn*, loved for the same exploring: an outcast heroine who knows little of her world uncovers its real history and her own origin together. Inferred, not his words: its world looks implausible (machine animals among tribes) and then earns a consistent explanation, the opposite of *Mortal Engines* 50, where the implausibility is only asserted.
- **Short idea-driven works have the character and emotional rules suspended — but the ideas still have to be good** (sharpened 2026-08-26; previously read as "judged on their ideas alone", which was taken as a free pass). The suspension **removes penalties, it never adds points.** Evidence for the suspension is solid: *The Little Prince* 82 (child lead), *Animal Farm* 78 (bleak), *The Silmarillion* 70 (no protagonist) each break a rule that costs other books points and none is penalised. But notice what those three share — each is canonical *for its ideas*. **The category looks safe because only its best examples have been read; that is selection bias, not a property of short fiction.** So there is no ceiling boost for being short, and a short book with thin ideas lands in the 60s like any other thin book. **Name the idea when proposing one — if you can't say what it is in a sentence, that is the finding**, and "it's short, so it's low risk" is not a case for reading it.
- **A collection is read from the front and put down on its weakest early stories — never averaged, and a bad story is not skipped** (new axis `axis:collection`, 2026-09-03). *Já, robot* 50 is the anchor and the log's first collection: abandoned after the third story, on the first story's cast and the third story's implausibility, without ever reaching the pieces the prediction had been built on. **Two things this kills.** A range set by a collection's best or most canonical stories is not a range, because the front of the book decides the outcome. And "the bad story is one of eight and is skippable" — the argument that held *Příběhy vašeho života* out of the 50s — is now known to be false about this reader. **What survives the axis best is an arc or a fix-up**, where each piece pays for the one before and the opening does what the rest does; **most exposed is a multi-author or best-of anthology**, where the order is an editor's and nothing earns patience. **Scope, stated: applied to the 28 collections at or above the propose line and to no others** — below the line it could only lower a number that already fails, which changes no decision. Same partial coverage `axis:sex-portrayal` carries.
- **Unearned competence is a turn-off** — ability and status *given* rather than earned; effortlessly good at everything. Evidence: *Divergent* 50 (Tris). Distinct from the messianic objection below, with which it was wrongly merged until 2026-08-22.
- **Prophecy, fate and chosen-one plots are graded on HANDLING, never on how much of the book they occupy** (prominence clause withdrawn 2026-10-24; never a filter since 2026-08-22). ***Licanius* 93-100 is the anchor, and it carries the heaviest prophecy load in the log**: Augurs who see the future, a foretold war, a fate the leads keep testing and a time loop closed in the last volume. The estimates docked it for exactly that ("the prophecy machinery gets heavier", held level), and the reader named it as a highlight instead: the characters question "the fate, believes, time in a way that make sense to the world, it's not forced or mocking anything". So **prominence costs nothing**, and **fate taken seriously inside the world's rules earns on the ideas axis**. What costs is the handling: **ridicule** (*Já, robot* 50, faith played as satire), **careless Jesus resonance** a book hasn't earned, and **foreknowledge that bends when the plot needs it**. That last one is the heaviest axis rather than this one: a declared power to see the future must obey its rules like any other. **The axis is reverence, not resemblance:** Aslan is the closest Christ figure fiction has and is *not* among Narnia 60's objections — "reverent allegory is fine". **Subversion is judged on tone:** a manufactured saviour played seriously is fine, played for cynicism is not. **Untested:** a *messianic* saviour prophecy at full prominence. Licanius is fate and foreknowledge, not a messiah, and "some prophecy about a saviour is ok" (2026-08-22) is all the log has there, so keep the reverence test for that case. Consequence: **never dock a book for how central its prophecy is**, and don't flag a Christ parallel merely for existing.
- **Verse does not work, whatever it is about — a new axis (2026-08-27).** *Havran* 10 is the lowest score in the log, and the reader named the form itself as the first of two causes. This is distinct from the short-idea-driven suspension: a short *prose* work merely fails to earn points, while verse carries a penalty of its own. The catalogue holds a great deal of poetry and nothing screened for it before this. Evidence: *Havran* 10.
- **Story density is the penalty term, not length** (corrected 2026-08-19). What costs is a thin story spread over many pages. *The Hobbit* 84 = *LOTR* 84 because LOTR's far greater ideas are cancelled by how sparse its story is for its length — length itself was explicitly *not* the objection. Consistent with *Monte Cristo* 60 and *Les Misérables* 71, both docked for digression, and stated outright on *Zaslaná pošta* 50: put down part-way because Pratchett "goes often off topic" with "quite some descriptive scenes". **Never discount a candidate for being long**; discount padding — description, digression, drawn-out endings (*The Martian* 82). A long, densely plotted book carries no penalty.
- **Sustained bleak emotional states are disliked, and no longer mildly** (resized on the 2026-10-29 rerank) — isolation, hopelessness, unrelieved grimness. *1984* is still the only book grim enough to matter, and "Too depressing" is its whole note, but it now sits at 50, level with the incoherent-premise and unrootable-lead books instead of at the top of the disliked band. *Proměna* 40 adds bleakness arriving at once to a passive lead. So price sustained despair like the other mid-severity objections. Still a graded deduction, not a bar, and it is the book's mood rather than its subject: *Animal Farm* 78 is bleak and distanced, something to think about rather than misery to sit in. Companionship and self-sacrifice beat solo survival: *The Martian* 82 (docked partly for loneliness) vs *PHM* 100 (Rocky, sacrifice plot) and *LOTR* 84 (heroism, friendship).
- **Faith and religion as a thread inside a story**, not as the whole of it — no theology or apologetics as a subject in its own right. Evidence: *Seeking Allah* 100 and *Son of Hamas* 60 carry faith inside a narrative; stated directly that a theology book is not wanted. **A second and heavier dimension, anchored for the first time 2026-09-03: religion played as satire is disliked outright.** *Já, robot* 50 was put down partly on a story whose robots invent a god and reason their way into believing in it — his words, "it seemed quite as satire on main religions, blind faith, which I did not like". Until today this clause existed only inside the prophecy bullet ("a treatment played for ridicule") with nothing scored behind it, and *Dobrá znamení* had been rejected on it; now it has a number. **The object is religion, not satire as such** — political and social satire is untouched, *Animal Farm* 78 being satire and sitting at 78 — and a *serious* treatment of belief, critical or devotional, is graded on the thread-versus-subject test above rather than here. **The serious side now has its own score:** in *Licanius* 93-100 the characters question belief and fate, and the reader praised it because it is "not forced or mocking anything". Same subject, opposite handling, 43 to 50 points above *Já, robot* 50. **Magnitude is not separable from the anchor:** that 50 carries two other causes, so this axis has a direction, no measured size, and the same unplaceable rank as `axis:same-sex-romance`.
- **The memoir/testimony form earns nothing by itself.** What earns is the examination of what is true plus insight into how another group experiences the world. *The Diary of a Young Girl* (DNF, unscored) (DNF) is the same form as *Seeking Allah* 100 and failed outright. *Son of Hamas* 60 is a second case: the same form, 40 under.
- **How much of a children's book it is — graded, never a filter** (ruled 2026-08-26: "children should not be a hard limit, it was just not to do screening from children books. The score depends how much children book it is"). The exclusion belongs to the **triage sweep**, which keeps the children's section out of the queue in the first place — `tools/ai-rank-prompt.md` still scores children's and early-reader rows a 1, and that is correct. But once a book is under consideration it gets a number: *Hobbit* 84 and *The Little Prince* 82 pay nothing; *Hraničářův učeň* (50-70 by volume, 70 at its best) and Narnia 60 pay more than the "a little" recorded before the 2026-10-29 rerank, though both also lack ideas, so the register's own share is not separable; and a picture book lands in the 30s or 40s. Two promotion agents hit this independently on 2026-08-26 — one declined to reject two middle-grade volumes because there was no `axis:childrens` id to spend, the other had to file a genuine picture book under `axis:life-phase` — which is what surfaced the question. `axis:childrens` now exists and is **the book's register and audience**, distinct from the life-phase pushback below, which is only the lead's age. A book can have a child lead in a wholly adult register, or an adult lead in a nursery one.

  **Screened deliberately on 2026-08-27, on the reader's request, and the axis behaves as stated.** The whole 6,779-record *literatura pro děti a mládež* heading was enumerated and the crossover seam promoted: 30 titles now carry estimates spanning **52 to 86**, which is a wider spread than any other single screen has produced, and the register deduction is what orders the bottom of it. Three findings worth keeping. **First, the deduction is not a constant — it scales with whether there is an adult layer for it to grade.** *Nekonečný příběh* and *Momo* pay almost nothing at 78-86 because what they are about is not childlike; *Medvídek Pú* pays heavily at 60-70 because the excellent jokes sit on nothing declared and nothing at stake; the age-banded serials that fill the shelf pay everything, which is why they are a category reject rather than a graded row. **Second, this category is the one place where a heading sweep is not sufficient on its own** — the library files the best of the crossover canon as adult prose, so *Nekonečný příběh*, *Momo*, *Sofiin svět*, *Alenka*, *Zlodějka knih*, *Robinson Crusoe* and *Huckleberry Finn* are all absent from the children's heading and were recovered only by name afterwards. **Third, the occult filter fires far more often here than anywhere else** — four title-level rejects in one pass, because a witch or a sorcerer's apprenticeship is a stock children's premise.

- **Protagonist life-phase proximity — a small pushback only** (reader is 28, 2026-08). Worth a couple of points, never a tier, and never a reason to reject. What counts is how the inner life reads, not the stated age: *Ender's Game* 87 marked down slightly because Ender *feels* like a child from the inside. No penalty for 16-year-olds who don't read as teenagers (*Red Rising* 95, *Hunger Games* 89), older leads (*LOTR* 84), or short allegory (*The Little Prince* 82).
- **Multi-POV openings are fine when the threads are staggered — the failure is simultaneity, not multiplicity** (sharpened 2026-08-19). A new line every few pages, or one per chapter, is welcome. Evidence: *Guards! Guards!* (DNF, unscored) (~6 threads by p. 15, all at once) vs *LOTR* 84 and *Golden Son* 92, each thread anchored before the next arrives. Two timelines decades apart cost nothing either: of *Problém tří těles* (DNF, unscored) he volunteered that the split "was not the problem".
- **Each character needs its own motives and way of thinking** (Darrow ≠ Sevro ≠ Mustang) — differentiation, explicitly *not* cast size. A larger cast is not itself a draw. Evidence: *Golden Son* 92.
- **Sequels are not safe bets** — a strong book 1 does not transfer. *Hitchhiker's* 83 → 60 (−23, steepest in the log); but Collins reverses it, *Catching Fire* 83 → *Mockingjay* 91. Score each entry. **What decides it is a new question, not a continuation** (sharpened 2026-10-24). A sequel that re-runs book 1's premise loses him: *Catching Fire*'s second arena was "arena for its own sake", and *Osudové nebe* was set aside in its first chapters, unscored, after *Počítání hvězd* 80, because "it seemed that the plot will be just simple continuation of the 1st one". A sequel that opens a new question holds or rises: *Mockingjay* 91 (war replaces the arena), *Licanius* 93 → 100 → 96 (each volume reveals what the last could not). So score a sequel on what it adds, and treat "more of book 1" as the risk, however well book 1 scored. An escalation of the same conflict is more of book 1, not a new question. **Reworded 2026-08-31 to carry the inheritance rule** in Filters above: this axis now decides exclusions as well as scores, so a sequel needs a `prereq` verdict before it needs a number. The two directions are independent — a weak book 1 does not exclude book 2, only an *excluded* book 1 does, and a strong book 1 still buys book 2 nothing.
- **Fast start and constant momentum.** Evidence: *Red Rising* 95, *Hunger Games* 89, *Ender's Game* 87. **Missing drive now has a read case** (2026-10-24). *Počítání hvězd* 80: premise and ideas liked, and the one named problem was not feeling the drive to read on. It did not keep that book below 8, but together with a sequel that looked like more of the same (see the sequels bullet), it is why the series stopped at volume 1. **A second case ended a book** (2026-10-29): *Problém tří těles*, dropped at p. 200 and left unscored, on "parts quite uneventful". It came together with a passive lead, though. So the two cases do not yet separate what missing drive costs on its own from what it costs when the lead cannot carry the slack.

## Open questions
- **RESOLVED, and the other way round from how it was first written (2026-08-28). The axis a prediction *doesn't* examine is the one that kills it — neither direction is the safe one.** This used to read "predictions built on prose, pacing or opening structure are weaker than predictions built on plausibility", on the single case of *Mortal Engines* 50: argued on its opening, lost on the world logic the prediction never looked at. *Čaropisci*, abandoned at chapter one, is that case exactly inverted — argued on worldbuilding and a rootable lead, lost on the opening, which the prediction never looked at either. Two reads, opposite leans, same shape of miss, so there is no weaker axis and no stronger one. **The operative rule: every estimate must state a verdict on both the opening and the world, and say which one it is leaning on.** (The Scalzi / Crouch / Taylor re-audit the old wording called for was **done 2026-08-22** — results are in their Author rows.)
- **A second prediction failure, different cause:** *Artemis* was predicted 80 and scored **60** (76 at first, 69 from 2026-08-27, 60 since the 2026-10-29 rerank), because the prediction leaned *up* on strong worldbuilding while the lead was visibly thin — a 20-point miss. Both of those failures come from predicting on the wrong axis, not from bad information.
- **A third prediction failure, and the first caused by `risks` rather than `leanedOn`:** *Proměna* was predicted [70, 78] and scored 40 — thirty points under since the 2026-10-29 rerank (58 and twelve under before), the largest miss in the log. The lean was right (`axis:ideas` + `axis:short-idea-driven`; the idea is real and nameable), but both cached risks fired together: `axis:protagonist` (Gregor is passive and hard to root for) and `axis:bleakness` (arriving at once rather than gradually). So the lesson is not "lean on a different axis" but **a range whose risks both fire is not a range at all** — price the downside, don't average it in. Evidence that the mechanism works: *Labyrint světa* 70 leaned on the identical pair and landed inside [66, 76] because its `axis:faith` risk did not fire. **Repeated 2026-10-29:** *Problém tří těles* [74, 84] → dropped at p. 200, left unscored as lost interest rather than dislike (first placed at 62). The lean (`axis:ideas`, `axis:discovery`) held, since the simulation was what he liked. `axis:protagonist` (a passive lead) and `axis:density` (uneventful stretches) fired. The third risk, `axis:multi-pov`, did not, and he said so unprompted. **Both of these misses had a passive lead sitting in `risks` next to a strong idea lean**, and both times the lead decided the outcome. - **The fifth prediction failure has REVERSED, and the reversal is more instructive than the failure was (rewritten 2026-09-02).** *Čaropisci* was predicted **[76, 84]**, recorded at **30** on a chapter-one DNF, then read to the end at **67**, lowered to **50** in the 2026-10-29 rerank. ## Authors

**Status is a prior on an author's *named* works, never a licence.** Established 2026-08-26 after four splits had to be made in a single day (Bennett, Čapek, Chiang, Hirsi Ali) and one false negative was found (Le Guin). Two rules:

- **Never propose a title because its author's row is `Top pick` or `Predicted fit`.** The title needs its own filter pass and its own worldbuilding verdict. Divine Cities was nearly proposed exactly this way — the author row said "Predicted fit — strong" and the book fails a hard filter.
- **Never let a title-specific objection downgrade the author.** Le Guin sat at `Downgraded` for a week over Earthsea's true-name spellcasting while *Levá ruka tmy* — society as subject, one declared premise worked out through politics, language and religion — sat unproposed on the shelf. Downgrade the work, scope the row. **Confirmed against a real score 2026-08-31, and on the objection most tempting to generalise:** Pratchett sat at `Avoid` on two DNF'd openings, with the row concluding "it's the prose style", while *Mort* 83 — same author, same series, same prose — had already been read and liked. An opening failure is title-level like every other objection, and two of them in one series is still not an author verdict.

**The real unit is the work, and the levels are author → series → volume → component.** The log's own within-author spread proves the author level is too coarse to predict with: Collins runs 91 → 50 (a 41-point spread inside one `Top pick`), Weir 100 → 60, Tolkien 84 → 60, Card 90 → 79, Adams 83 → 60. Those gaps are wider than the distance between `Top pick` and `Fine, low` as categories. Bennett needed three verdicts for three series; *Příběhy vašeho života* needed flags on two individual stories. **Every Status cell must say what it covers** — "Ender canon only", "Founders yes, Divine Cities no" — and a row without a scope is a bug, not a shorthand.

## Access-checking methods

**Search YouTube for a full reading.** Judge the titles, don't count hits — results mix full readings with summaries, abridgements and AI narration.
```
curl -sL -A "Mozilla/5.0" "https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=<title>+<author>+full+audiobook" \
 | grep -oE '"videoId":"[^"]{11}"|"title":\{"runs":\[\{"text":"[^"]{10,140}"' \
 | sed 's/"videoId":"/ID /;s/"$//;s/.*"text":"/T /' \
 | awk '/^ID /{id=$2} /^T /{sub(/^T /,""); if(id!=""&&!seen[id]++){print id" | "$0}}' \
 | grep -iE 'audio ?book' | grep -viE 'summary|recap|review' | head -4
```

**Always duration-check before giving a link out** (learned 2026-08-19, four dead links in one pass).
```
curl -sL -A "Mozilla/5.0" "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<ID>" | grep -oE '"lengthSeconds":"[0-9]+"' | head -1
```
Uploads titled "Full Audiobook Free" are routinely 1-5 minute stubs — that channel style is the tell, and re-checks on 2026-08-19 and 2026-08-22 found every such hit for Mistborn, The Will of the Many, Recursion and We Are Legion still stubbed. Anything under ~60 % of published runtime is abridged or AI-compressed — say so rather than linking it plain. Full uploads are usually found instead by searching `<title> <author> audiobook part 1`, or by the narrator's name.

**Catalogue API gotcha: strip the diacritics.** A query whose first significant word carries a diacritic returns zero hits — `Tři mušketýři`, `Půl krále`, `Jurský park`, `Temná hmota` all came back empty while the books are on the shelf; `Tri musketyri`, `Pul krale`, `Jursky park`, `Temna hmota` all hit. Author surname alone also works. Never treat a diacritic query's `NO HITS` as absence.
```
curl -sL --get --data-urlencode "q=Jursky park" "https://katalog.mekvalmez.cz/api/search"
```
Read `result.content[].name` plus `responsibilityStatement.text` — that field distinguishes a translation, a comic adaptation and an audio edition. Check the *edition*: an audiobook-only publisher (Témbr, OneHotBook, Tympanum) can mean no print copy.

**The other fields worth reading, verified against a live record 2026-08-26:** `publishers[0].text` (the whole "V Praze : Odeon, 2008" string, place and year included), `publicationStartYear.value`, `isbns[0].text`, and `id` — the record UUID, which is the borrow link (`/records/<id>`). Guessing at `publisher` or `year` as flat keys returns empty strings and looks like missing data.

**Detect an audio edition on `čtou`, not only `čte`** (learned 2026-08-26). A two-narrator record reads "čtou Josef Somr a Magdalena Sidonová", and a checker matching only the singular read Hrabal's *Pábitelé* — which this library holds solely as a Popron audiobook — as a print copy. Match `čt[eo]u?`, `načet`, and the audio publishers.

**Correction 2026-08-26 — the API paginates fine; the old note had the parameter names wrong.** `page` / `size` do return HTTP 500, but **`pageNumber` and `pageSize` work**, `pageSize` up to **100** (150 and above → 500). `sorting` accepts the ids in `availableSortings`: `relevance`, `PNAZEV` (title), `REZS_AUTOR`, `REZS_ROK` (oldest first), `-REZS_ROK` (newest first). `q` supports boolean `AND` and trailing wildcards. `result.totalElements` / `totalPages` give the real hit count. So the catalogue is sweepable, not just checkable — "query narrowly instead of paginating" was a self-imposed limit, not an API one. Facet ids exist (`REZS_PREDMET` keywords, `REZS_DESKRIPTOR` genre, `REZS_AUTOR`, `REZS_JAZYK`, `REZS_FOND` type) but values come back empty and `requestedFacets` 500s. The CSV/XLS export (`/export?target=…&object=…`, capped at 400 records) returns **HTTP 504** even with a session cookie — treat as unavailable.
```
curl -sL -A "Mozilla/5.0" --get --data-urlencode "q=<term>" \
  --data-urlencode "pageSize=100" --data-urlencode "pageNumber=<n>" \
  --data-urlencode "sorting=-REZS_ROK" "https://katalog.mekvalmez.cz/api/search"
```

**Discovery sweep — use the catalogue to *find* candidates, not only to confirm them** (added 2026-08-26). Name-first searching only ever confirms books already thought of, so it inherits the blind spots of the Author table. Two axes that actually yield:
- **Publisher-scoped, because Czech imprints sort themselves by audience.** Adult/translated SF-F: *Triton* / *Planeta9*, *Laser-books*, *Argo*, *Baronet*, *Návrat*, *Polaris*, *Host*. Skip for this reader: *Fragment*, *CooBoo*, *Dobrovský*, *Mystery Press*, *Slovart* romantasy (crude-sexual-content risk is central, not incidental) and *Crew* (manga, wrong format).
- **Theme keywords tied to the profile, not the genre** — `první kontakt`, `kolonizace vesmíru`, `utopie`, `totalitní`, `svědectví`. Genre terms plus `-REZS_ROK` mostly return the current YA/romantasy intake; sort `REZS_ROK` or scope by publisher to reach the canon instead.
